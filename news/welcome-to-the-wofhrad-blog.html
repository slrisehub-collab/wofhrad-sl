Welcome to the WOFHRAD blog

16 July 2013

What is WOFHRAD?

The Women’s Forum for Human Rights and Democracy (otherwise known as WOFHRAD), is based in Makeni, Sierra Leone. Our aim is to ensure the promotion and protection of human rights, justice and democracy with emphasis on the rights of women and girls. We organise activities and training for women and go into schools to educate children on the rights of women. We are also currently involved in monitoring some of the local courts to make sure they run as they should. The ultimate aim of WOFHRAD is to promote a culture of peace.

When and why was WOFHRAD founded?

WOFHRAD-SL is a young organization, founded in 2006 by a group of young women. All the core founding members of WOFHRAD-SL during that period were creative students of the University of Makeni and were greatly influenced by the Vice Chancellor Rev. Fr. DR. Joe Turay during this period, he was encouraging students that the university was ‘Not grooming job seekers but job creators’. At the same time Ms. Isha Kanu-Moriba was also working with the United Nations Assistant Mission in Sierra Leone and was visiting communities where she was seeing women being deprived of their basic human rights. She always desired to do something to improve lives of the girl child and women in Sierra Leone. She decided to bring up these ideas to set up an organization with a group of women as a way of combating the majority of those problems women and the girl child are experiencing in the northern region. Ms. Isha Kanu-Moriba, unanimously with all co-founders of WOFHRAD-SL, have fulfilled the hope of their Vice Chancellor not only by creating jobs for others but by bringing some positive change to society.

What have WOFHRAD done so far?

After registering with the Ministry of Social Welfare, Gender and Childrens Affairs, WOFHRAD secured funding from the Justice and Peace Commision to go into secondary schools and teach the pupils about the ‘Truth and Reconciliation Report’ (which looked at the causes of the war and made recommendations which created lots of development programmes). After the report was published many people didn’t know what it was or even that it existed. WOFHRAD went to 10 secondary schools in Makeni to tell the pupils and teachers about the report and what it meant and to ask them to spread the information to their families.

The next project was ‘Facilitating Access to Justice for Sexual and Gender Based Violence (SGBV) victims’ which was funded by the Justice Sector Development Programme. This project was run in the Safroka-Limba Cheifdom in the Bombali District of Sierra Leone. The staff of WOFHRAD helped victims of SGBV with the cost of transportation which allowed them to get from their homes in a rural part of the country to the bigger towns to go to the police stations and attend court. This meant that women who would normally have not been able to report crimes perpetrated against them were able to seek justice.

WOFHRAD then obtained funding from ENCISS to run training and outreach sessions in all 13 chiefdoms of Bombali District during the National Election. This project was aimed at young people and included training on peace building, the causes and effects of violence and why they should not get involved with any violence surrounding the elections.

Currently WOFHRAD have a 1 year project with Action Aid International. It focuses on womens rights to land. There will be more on these projects in a later blog entry.
