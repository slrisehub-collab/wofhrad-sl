WOMEN’S FORUM FOR HUMAN RIGHTS AND DEMOCRACY(WOFHRAD) EMBARKS ON A NATIONWIDE LEADERSHIP TRAINING FOR WOMEN CANDIDATES IN SIERRA LEONE

01 June 2023

In collaboration with the UN Peace Building Fund, UN Women, UNDP, World Vision, and the Government of Sierra Leone, the Women’s Forum for Human Rights and Democracy is making considerable progress toward empowering women politicians in Sierra Leone and fostering long-term peace. Given the continued difficulties facing the nation, the workshop’s theme, “Localization and Working for Peace: Repositioning Women-Focused CSOs for Sustainable Peace in Sierra Leone,” is particularly pertinent.

By bringing together women politicians from various regions, including Karene, Pujehun, Bombali, Kono, Western Rural, and Western Urban, the workshop aims to create a platform for sharing experiences, exchanging ideas, and developing strategies for building a peaceful and prosperous Sierra Leone. Through this workshop, women-focused CSOs will be better equipped to support women in politics and promote their participation in decision-making processes at all levels.

Overall, the workshop is a significant opportunity to strengthen the role of women in promoting sustainable peace and development in Sierra Leone. The knowledge, skills, and networks that women politicians will gain through this workshop will be valuable in advancing the agenda of gender equality and inclusive governance in the country.

When discussing the importance of providing training for female candidates at the national and district levels with the WOFHRAD Programme Coordinator, Mrs. Emilia Kamara Jengo, in Karene, she used the following essential concepts:

1. Empowerment: Training can empower women to participate more fully in the political process by providing them with the skills and knowledge needed to run for office.

2. Networking: Training programs can allow women to network with other women leaders and potential campaign supporters, which can help build a solid support base for their campaigns.

3. Skill Development: Women candidates may need more specific skills or experience to run a successful campaign, such as fundraising, public speaking, and media relations. Training can help them develop these skills and gain the confidence needed to be successful.

4. Increased Representation: More women in politics can lead to better representation of women’s issues and perspectives in government.

5. Breaking Stereotypes: We can challenge stereotypes and biases that have historically limited women’s political participation by training and promoting women candidates.

The themes that emerged from participant feedback included increased confidence in their abilities as candidates, improved knowledge of campaign strategies and tactics, and a greater sense of community and support among fellow women candidates. Participants may also express gratitude for the opportunity to learn from experienced women leaders and to build long-lasting connections with other women in politics.
